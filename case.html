Overview

How do you guide enterprise users through a product that does everything?

The platform had over 40 features. New users were expected to figure it out on their own. Activation rates were dropping and support tickets were climbing. We had 8 weeks to fix onboarding.

My role was to lead the full design process — from discovery to a tested, developer-ready prototype.

Problem

Users were dropping before they saw the value

Data showed that 62% of new users never completed their first meaningful action. Most left within the first session, overwhelmed by choice with no clear starting point.

Research

Understanding the gap between what we built and how people think

We ran 12 user interviews across 3 customer segments — ops managers, finance leads, and IT admins. We also analysed session recordings and mapped where users were exiting.

Affinity map — research synthesis Replace with actual image

Three patterns emerged: users didn't understand the product's mental model, couldn't tell what was optional vs. required, and had no sense of progress during setup.

Process

From 23 ideas to one testable prototype in three weeks

We ran a design sprint to generate and pressure-test directions quickly. I built three concepts and tested them with 6 users before committing to one direction.

Week 3–4Ideation sprint — HMW exercises, sketches, 3 prototype directions
Week 5–6Usability testing — 6 participants, 2 rounds, major pivot on step 3

Solution

A role-based onboarding that meets users where they are

Instead of a generic walkthrough, we built a short intake flow that identifies the user's role and primary goal. The dashboard then surfaces only what's relevant — hiding complexity until users are ready.

We also added a persistent progress indicator and a "quick win" prompt on the first session — a small task designed to show value in under 5 minutes.

Outcomes

Shipped, measured, and still improving

The new onboarding shipped 3 months after kickoff. Results came in within the first 30 days.

+38%

increase in first-session activation rate

−41%

drop in onboarding-related support tickets

The core learning: enterprise users don't need fewer features — they need a clearer path to the one that matters right now. Progressive disclosure, not simplification.
